Data protection and compliance

Your documents stay on your own server

Uploaded documents, results and audit records all stay on a server you run yourself, with no cloud service needed. The AI features are off by default, and once turned on they only send content to the server you choose. This makes it easier for your organization to meet requirements for personal data protection, information security and AI management.

Taiwan's Personal Data Protection Act, GDPR, ISO/IEC 27001:2022 and ISO/IEC 42001:2023 assess how an organization manages, not a piece of software, so this tool does not claim to "pass" or "comply with" any certification. This page explains what the tool itself does and what your organization still needs to do after deploying it, as a reference for building your management system and preparing for audits. It is not legal advice.

What the tool does

These controls are built into the software and work once it is installed and configured. Each card lists the related articles and controls, and the automated tests that verify it.

Access control

  • Sign-in: local accounts, LDAP, Active Directory, and OIDC and SAML single sign-on, with optional two-step verification; repeated failed sign-ins lock the account automatically.
  • Permissions: each tool is granted through roles, which can be assigned to individuals, groups or AD organizational units.
  • Isolation: uploaded files, job results, workspaces and drafting cases are visible only to their owners; guessing IDs to reach other people's data is always refused.
  • Segregation of duties: auditors can only view records and cannot use the tools; form filling, stamp and watermark history files can be viewed only by auditors.

What your organization does

The tool provides the features; your organization puts the controls into practice. Go through these items when you deploy it.

Deployment and network

  • HTTPS: provide HTTPS through a reverse proxy and keep the service itself bound to the local machine (the installer default).
  • Network: keep the server on your internal network and use a firewall to limit who can connect.
  • Connected services: keep the LLM, GPU OCR and speech servers on your internal network; before using an outside service, review how it keeps and uses data.
  • Host security: system updates, account management and disk encryption.

Accounts and permissions

  • Turn on authentication: connect your company directory or single sign-on so accounts change along with the directory.
  • Roles: set roles and tool permissions by job; create auditor accounts separately and turn on two-step verification for them.
  • Leavers and transfers: disable the account; directory sync can disable accounts that are disabled in the directory automatically.
  • Access reviews: keep the role and permission settings of each review with a settings backup export.

Retention and backups

  • Retention periods: set the retention period for each kind of data according to laws and internal policy.
  • Audit log: forward it to a SIEM for long-term retention.
  • Backups: copy the database and settings backups to another host, and rehearse restores regularly.
  • Change management: before upgrading, read the notes for that version in CHANGELOG.md and keep them as a record.

AI use and human review

  • Usage policy: decide which tools may be used and which kinds of documents may go to which model server.
  • Human review: AI-produced documents, summaries and translations are used only after the responsible person has checked them.
  • Retest before changing models: before changing models or upgrading, rerun your own representative documents and keep the results.
  • Impact assessment: for each use, assess who could be affected by errors (recipients of documents, the people the personal data is about, employees).
